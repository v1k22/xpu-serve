# 0013: LFM3

Status: **accepted** · 2026-11-11

## Context

LFM3 (Liquid AI; `LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B`, `model_type` `lfm3`) is a hybrid decoder: of its 24 layers, 13 are gated short
convolutions (one projection gives two gates and a signal; a causal depthwise convolution of seven taps runs between
the gates), 6 attend over the whole context and 5 over a window of 256 tokens. The attention has q/k norms and a
sigmoid gate per head; full layers rotate the first half of each head (at the frequencies of the whole head), sliding
layers all of it. Every operator and MLP output is normed again before it joins the residual stream, and the head is
tied to a 128,000-row embedding. The context is 256k tokens. transformers 5.22 has no class for it: the reference is
the modeling code in the checkpoint.

## Decision

- **A folder of its own, `models/lfm3/`, built from the shared blocks; no new graph op.** The convolution is
  `CausalConv` (the op Qwen3.5's linear attention uses, here with seven taps and no SiLU after it), the half-rotated
  heads are `rope_inv_freq`'s zero tail (as Gemma 4's proportional RoPE), the sliding layers are the attention window.
- **One addition to `blocks::attention`**: `AttentionParts::head_gate`, a projection of the layer's input to one value
  per head whose sigmoid scales that head's output.
- **The output dialect is `qwen3_5`.** LFM3 writes a turn with the same markers (`<think>`, `<tool_call>` blocks of
  `<function=NAME>` and `<parameter=KEY>`), with nothing around its reasoning and its calls exactly as its template
  renders them. A parsed reply rendered back is the text the model wrote, so a tool result extends the cached
  sequence.
- **The tokenizer needed nothing new**: byte-level BPE with the Llama 3 Split regex and `ignore_merges`.
- **The checkpoint's chat template and tokenizer are not copied into this repository** (they come under the LFM Open
  License v1.0, which limits commercial use). Their tests read them from a checkpoint (`XS_LFM3_DIR`) and compare
  with Hugging Face outputs that are committed (`tests/fixtures/chat/lfm3_cases.json`,
  `tests/fixtures/tokenizer/lfm3_cases.json`); without a checkpoint they skip.
- **Refused by name**: `conv_bias`, `use_sandwich_norm: false`, an untied head, a RoPE type other than `default`.

## What was measured

Against Hugging Face fp32 (the checkpoint's own code, CPU), six prompts of 5 to 1,770 tokens, last-token logits and
32 greedy tokens (`tests/unit/model/model_parity.cpp`):

| Checkpoint | Backend | Logits cosine | Max logit error | Greedy tokens equal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tiny (8 layers, window 16) | cpu-ref f32 | 1 | 1.3e-5 | 32 of 32, all prompts |
| tiny | Arc Pro B60 f32 | 1 | 8e-6 | 32 of 32 |
| tiny | Arc Pro B60 f16 | 0.9998 to 0.99999 | 0.043 | 32 of 32 on five prompts, 27 on one |
| LFM3-1.7B | cpu-ref f32 | 1 | 2.4e-5 | 32 of 32 |
| LFM3-1.7B | Arc Pro B60 f32 | 1 | 6e-5 | 32 of 32 |
| LFM3-1.7B | Arc Pro B60 f16 | 1 | 0.013 | 32 of 32 |
| LFM3-1.7B | Arc Pro B60 f16, int8 KV | 0.99998 | 0.15 | 32 of 32 |
| LFM3-1.7B q8 | Arc Pro B60 f16 | 0.9998 | 0.35 | 32 of 32 on five prompts, 17 on one |
| LFM3-1.7B q4 (g32) | Arc Pro B60 f16 | 0.979 to 0.995 | 4.1 | 5 to 32 |

The tiny checkpoint's RoPE bases are small (200 and 50) and its head gates large: at the released base of 1e6 the
unrotated half of a head hardly differs from a rotated one within a prompt, and a constant gate vanishes in the norm
after the attention, so a wrong layout or a missing gate would pass. AWQ (`convert --method awq`) runs on the model
and leaves the q4 logits where round to nearest does (cosine 0.977 to 0.997).

Arc Pro B60, `bench`, 128 to 256 generated tokens:

| Prompt tokens | f16 decode | q8 | q4 | Prefill (f16) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,024 | 99 tok/s | 159 | 206 | 13,500 tok/s (75 ms) |
| 4,096 | 98 | 158 | 202 | 12,700 (0.32 s) |
| 16,384 | 91 | 139 | 170 | 10,000 (1.6 s) |
| 65,536 | 68 | 93 | 107 | 5,200 (12.6 s) |

Packs: 3.46 GB to 1.78 GB (q8) and 1.20 GB (q4; the embedding table, which is also the head, stays q8).
MMLU-Pro through the server (25 questions, thinking on, greedy, at most 4,096 tokens an answer): 60 % at f16, 64 % at
q8, 52 % at q4 and 60 % at q4 with AWQ. A sample of 25 does not separate these; what does differ is how many answers
ran into the token limit: 1, 0, 4 and 5.
Through the server at f16: thinking, streaming, two tool calls in one turn, a tool result (197 of 225 prompt tokens
from the cache), and retrieval from long prompts: three of three code words at 16k tokens, two of three at 57k (the
miss, the same at f32 precision, names item 160 for item 1650).

## Consequences

- LFM3's state is the KV of its attention layers and the last six inputs of each convolution. A conv slot cannot be
  cut back to a prefix, so a prompt that does not extend the cached sequence is prefilled again. With thinking on the
  template drops the reasoning of earlier turns, so every new user turn is such a prompt (12,000 tok/s on the B60);
  with thinking off, and across tool calls, the cache is reused.
- q8 is the pack to serve: it decodes 1.6 times faster than f16 and stays close to the reference (logits cosine
  0.9998, 32 greedy tokens equal on five of six prompts). q4 is 1.3 times faster again, but its logits drift and its
  reasoning runs long more often.
- The vision and audio variants (`lfm3_vl`, `lfm3_audio`, `lfm3_omni`) are other model types: refused.
- No drafter exists for it; `--spec` fails by name.
